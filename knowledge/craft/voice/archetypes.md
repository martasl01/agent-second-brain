# Craft — Voice Archetypes

The case study uses **9 voice archetypes** to match tone to content. Pick one per post *before* drafting — the archetype sets the stance, the verbs, and what counts as evidence. The default is **Builder-Teacher** (Rule 2 in `../writing-techniques.md`: it outperforms the Analyst take).

> **Provenance:** The *count (9)* and the *Builder-Teacher > Analyst* finding are **[from case study]**. The specific archetype definitions below are **starters** — the article does not publish the 9. Refine them against your own voice as you log performance.

---

## How to use

1. Name the archetype at the top of the draft.
2. Write to its stance and verbs.
3. If two archetypes fit, prefer the one higher in this list — they're ordered by how often they should win, with Builder-Teacher first by rule.

---

## The 9 (STARTER)

### 1. Builder-Teacher — *default* **[high performer, from case study]**
*Stance:* "I built this; here's what I learned so you can too."
*Verbs:* built, shipped, broke, fixed, learned.
*Evidence:* the artifact, the number, the before/after.
*Use when:* you did the thing. Which should be most of the time.

### 2. Documentarian
*Stance:* "Here's exactly what happened, step by step."
*Use when:* the process itself is the value (a teardown, a play-by-play).

### 3. Experimenter
*Stance:* "I tried X to test Y. Here's the result."
*Use when:* there's a clean experiment — pairs with the data-experiment format (Rule 1).

### 4. Contrarian
*Stance:* "Everyone says X. The data says otherwise."
*Use when:* you have evidence against a common belief — cross-check `hypotheses/rejected.md` so you're fighting a real false belief, not a strawman.

### 5. Synthesizer
*Stance:* "Three things just happened. Here's the pattern."
*Use when:* connecting recent events into one frame.

### 6. Mentor
*Stance:* "If I were starting over, here's what I'd do."
*Use when:* the audience is earlier in the journey than you.

### 7. Storyteller
*Stance:* "Let me tell you about the time…"
*Use when:* a concrete narrative carries the lesson better than bullets.

### 8. Curator
*Stance:* "Here are the few things worth your attention, and why."
*Use sparingly:* lists age fast and earn shallow saves; add a clear point of view.

### 9. Analyst — *use with caution* **[lower performer, from case study]**
*Stance:* "Here's my read on what this means."
*Use when:* the post is explicitly a landscape/synthesis piece **and** no builder angle exists. Per Rule 2, prefer Builder-Teacher whenever you actually did the thing.

---

## Voice hygiene

- One archetype per post. Mixing reads as no voice at all.
- If you keep reaching for Analyst, ask whether you could have *built* the thing instead — that usually makes a better post (Rule 2).
- Track which archetypes win in `../posts/performance-log.md`; let evidence reshape this list.
