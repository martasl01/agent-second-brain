# Platform — LinkedIn

Platform-specific rules, hooks, and templates for LinkedIn. These **override** the generic craft rules in `../writing-techniques.md` where they conflict. Load alongside writing-techniques when drafting for LinkedIn.

> **Provenance:** Rule 1 (negation > positive) is **[from case study]** — reported with data. The rest are **starters**.

---

## Rules

### Rule 1 — Negation hooks beat positive hooks **[from case study]**
On LinkedIn, hooks framed as a negation ("Stop doing X", "X is not the problem", "Nobody tells you that…") outperform positive/affirmative hooks ("Here's how to X", "5 ways to Y").
*Implication:* draft the hook as a negation first; only fall back to positive framing if the negation feels forced.
*Evidence:* reported in the case study (graduated — see `../hypotheses/index.md` HYP-001).

### Rule 2 — Front-load before the "…see more" fold (starter)
LinkedIn truncates after ~2–3 lines. The hook **and** the reason to expand must live above the fold. No throat-clearing.

### Rule 3 — Whitespace is structure (starter)
Short lines, frequent breaks. Dense paragraphs die in the feed. One idea per line in the body.

---

## Hook patterns (negation-first, per Rule 1)

- "Everyone tells you to {common advice}. It's backwards."
- "{Popular tactic} didn't move the needle. {Unexpected thing} did."
- "You don't have a {X} problem. You have a {Y} problem."
- "Nobody warns you about {hidden cost of the obvious path}."
- "I stopped doing {widely-recommended thing}. {Metric} went up."

## Templates

### Data-experiment post (highest value — Rule 1 of writing-techniques)
```
[Negation hook tied to the result]

I tried [smallest version of the experiment].

Expected: [the common assumption]
Got: [the actual number / before→after]

Why: [one-sentence mechanism]

[One-line takeaway the reader can apply today]
```

### Builder-teacher post
```
[Negation hook]

Last week I built [thing]. Here's what I'd tell myself before starting:

— [lesson 1, concrete]
— [lesson 2, concrete]
— [lesson 3, concrete]

[The one idea, restated as a takeaway]
```

## Avoid
- Positive-platitude hooks (violates Rule 1).
- Broetry for its own sake — whitespace serves the idea, it isn't the idea.
- Engagement-bait ("Agree? 👇") — see `../writing-techniques.md` anti-patterns.
